# AI Policy as Pedagogy

Co-developing AI course policy with students  
Parsa Rajabi, UBC | AI-cademy 2026

[Workshop slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1DlYS9xmkcGOBZjEglxsfDFX6Nx7E7Ps0JPp1kVb8B8A/present) | [Miro board](https://miro.com/app/board/uXjVEQJSf7A=/?share_link_id=119899897790) (tiny.cc/aicademy-miro)

## Run the activity (15 min)

1. **Set the context (2 min):** Name the assessment and what students are learning. Explain what's fixed and what students get to shape. Model one card: a student works on an assignment for 20 minutes, then asks AI for a hint. Yellow condition: they can explain what they tried and where they got stuck.
2. **Discuss in pairs or trios (8 min):** Groups pick a frame on the board and mark it "In use". For each card, keep one colour and delete the others (green = allowed, yellow = with conditions, red = not allowed). Say yellow conditions out loud. Early finishers can try the extra cards on the left.
3. **Compare (5 min):** Start with card 2. Which colour did groups pick, and what did they think it was assessing? Have two groups that disagreed explain. Write down one condition students can actually apply.

> [!TIP]
> No devices? Print the cards and have students circle one colour per card.

## The four cards

1. **Studying:** You give AI your own lecture notes. It turns them into flashcards. You try to answer each one before flipping it over.
2. **A graded assignment:** You use AI to create part of a graded assignment. You can independently explain, verify, and change everything it produced, but without AI you probably would not have produced that part yourself in the time available.
3. **Writing:** You draft an explanation and ask AI to flag where a reader might get lost. You revise it yourself. Would your decision change if AI rewrote the paragraph?
4. **Presenting:** You draw a diagram showing how a campus printer decides whose document prints next. You ask AI to play the audience and ask questions while you practise. What if AI wrote your speaking notes?

## Adapt it to your course

- **Assessment and learning goal:** Students will demonstrate ___ by ___.
- **Fixed boundaries:** The course requires ___ and prohibits ___, because ___.
- **Student input:** Where could the class reasonably choose between two approaches?
- **A yellow condition:** On ___, students may use AI to ___ only if ___.
- **Disclosure:** What should students record, where, and in how much detail?
- **Follow-through:** Share a draft, take corrections, and publish the final wording. Give students who missed the activity a way to weigh in. Revisit it mid-term.

## Personal AI policy

Pick one upcoming assignment. Revisit after you submit.

- **Green:** I will use AI for ___ because ___.
- **Yellow:** I will use AI for ___ only if ___.
- **Red:** I will do ___ myself because I want to practise ___.
