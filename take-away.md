This page has the activity from the workshop so you can run it with your own students. It takes about 15 minutes of class time.

## Running the Activity

### 1. Set the context (2 min)

Name the assessment and what students are learning from it. Explain any fixed rules, and which decisions students get to shape. Then walk through one card together. For example, a student works on an assignment for 20 minutes and then asks AI for a hint. One possible yellow condition is that the student can explain what they tried and where they got stuck.

### 2. Discuss in pairs or trios (8 min)

Each group shares one device, picks an open group frame on the board, and changes its status to "In use". For each card, they keep one colour and delete the other two.

| Colour | Meaning |
| :----- | :------ |
| Green | Allowed |
| Yellow | Allowed with conditions |
| Red | Not allowed |

If a group picks yellow, they should say the condition out loud. Groups that finish early can discuss the extra cards on the left side of the board.

> [!TIP]
> Prefer paper? Print the cards and have groups circle one colour per card.

### 3. Compare decisions (5 min)

Start with card 2. Which colour did each group pick, and what did they assume the assignment was assessing? Ask two groups with different answers to explain their reasoning. Then write down one condition in words a student could actually apply.

## The Four Cards

Feel free to copy these into your own course.

1. Studying: You give AI your own lecture notes. It turns them into flashcards. You try to answer each one before flipping it over.
2. A graded assignment: You use AI to create part of a graded assignment. You can independently explain, verify, and change everything it produced, but without AI you probably would not have produced that part yourself in the time available.
3. Writing: You draft an explanation and ask AI to flag where a reader might get lost. You revise it yourself. Would your decision change if AI rewrote the paragraph?
4. Presenting: You draw a diagram showing how a campus printer decides whose document prints next. You ask AI to play the audience and ask questions while you practise. What if AI wrote your speaking notes?

## Adapting It to Your Course

Pick one assessment and fill in the blanks.

- Assessment and learning goal: Students will demonstrate ___ by ___.
- Fixed boundaries: The course requires ___ and prohibits ___. Why?
- Where students get a say: Where could the class reasonably choose between two approaches?
- One yellow condition: On ___, students may use AI to ___ only if ___. Include a concrete example.
- Disclosure: What should students record, where, and how much detail is actually useful?
- Follow-through: Share a draft with the class, ask for corrections, and publish the final wording. Let students who missed the activity know how they can still have a say. Pick a date around the middle of the term to revisit the policy.

## Personal AI Policy

Pick one upcoming assignment and finish these sentences. Come back to them after you submit.

- Green: I will use AI for ___ because ___.
- Yellow: I will use AI for ___ only if ___.
- Red: I will do ___ myself because I want to practise ___.
