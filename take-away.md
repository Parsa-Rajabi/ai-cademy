# AI Policy as Pedagogy
## Co-developing AI course policy with students

## Run the activity in 15 minutes
1. **Set the context (2 minutes).** Name the assessment and what students are learning. Explain any fixed rules and which decisions students can shape. Demonstrate one card: after trying an assignment for 20 minutes, a student asks AI for a hint. One possible yellow condition is that the student can explain what they tried and where they got stuck.
2. **Discuss in pairs or trios (8 minutes).** Share a device, choose an available group frame and change its status to “In use.” Read each card, keep one colour and delete the other two. Green means allowed, yellow means allowed with conditions, and red means prohibited. Say each yellow condition aloud. Early finishers can discuss the extra cards on the left. For a paper version, circle one colour per card.
3. **Compare decisions (5 minutes).** Start with card 2. Which colour did groups choose? What did they assume the assignment was assessing? Ask two groups with different answers for their reasoning. Record one condition in language a student could apply.

## Four cards to copy into your own course
1. **Studying.** You give AI your own lecture notes. It turns them into flashcards. You try to answer each one before flipping it over.
2. **A graded assignment.** You use AI to create part of a graded assignment. You can independently explain, verify, and change everything it produced, but without AI you probably would not have produced that part yourself in the time available.
3. **Writing.** You draft an explanation and ask AI to flag where a reader might get lost. You revise it yourself. Would your decision change if AI rewrote the paragraph?
4. **Presenting.** You draw a diagram showing how a campus printer decides whose document prints next. You ask AI to play the audience and ask questions while you practise. What if AI wrote your speaking notes?

## Course adaptation worksheet
- **Assessment and learning goal:** Students will demonstrate ___ by ___.
- **Fixed boundaries:** The course requires ___ and prohibits ___. Explain why.
- **Student input:** Where could the class reasonably choose between two approaches?
- **A yellow condition:** On ___, students may use AI to ___ only if ___. Give one concrete example.
- **Disclosure:** What should students record, where, and how much detail will help?
- **Follow-through:** Share a draft, invite corrections, and publish the agreed wording. Explain how students who missed the activity can contribute. Set a midterm date to revisit the policy.

## Personal AI policy
Complete these sentences for one upcoming assignment. Revisit them after submission.
- Green: I will use AI for ___ because ___.
- Yellow: I will use AI for ___ only if ___.
- Red: I will do ___ myself because I want to practise ___.
